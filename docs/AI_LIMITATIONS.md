# AI Limitations in Cyber Threat Hunting & Security Operations

While Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLMs) significantly accelerate alert triage and narrative synthesis, their deployment in critical Security Operations Centers (SOC) introduces substantial risks that require formal architectural constraints.

---

## 1. Hallucination and Fabricated Threat Indicators

### The Vulnerability:
LLMs trained on internet corpora are probabilistic text completion engines. When an analyst asks an unconstrained prompt like *"Identify any lateral movement IPs"*, an LLM may fabricate plausible-looking IP addresses (`192.168.1.150`), fictitious CVE numbers (`CVE-2024-99999`), or imaginary malware signatures that do not exist in the raw logs. If an automated firewall script acts on hallucinated IoCs, it can block legitimate business partners or domain controllers.

### AegisHunt Defensive Mitigation:
**Deterministic Evidence Linking:**  
Our platform decouples inference from raw data retrieval. Every claim generated by the AI is programmatically anchored to an ingested `event_uuid` (e.g. `[EVT-APT-004]`). Clicking any citation loads the exact cryptographic JSON payload. If a claim lacks an associated database record, the platform flags it as ungrounded.

---

## 2. Base-Rate Fallacy & False Positive Cascades

### The Vulnerability:
In an enterprise network producing 100,000,000 events daily, true malicious events constitute less than 0.0001% of total traffic. Even an AI model boasting 99.5% classification accuracy will generate **500,000 false positive alerts every single day**. This causes severe alert fatigue and causes human analysts to ignore valid alerts.

### AegisHunt Defensive Mitigation:
**Multi-Domain Correlation Gating:**  
The platform does not alert on individual anomalies. Isolated PowerShell executions or port sweeps remain suppressed as baseline events until correlated across **multiple independent orthogonal domains** (Endpoint + Authentication + Network + Cloud) within a sliding time window.

---

## 3. Adversarial Evasion and Telemetry Poisoning

### The Vulnerability:
Sophisticated advanced persistent threats (APTs) actively engineer evasion against AI models:
- **Living-off-the-Land (LotL):** Adversaries abuse native binaries (`certutil`, `wmic`, `bitsadmin`, `powershell`) with benign flag combinations.
- **Log Flooding / Noise Injection:** Adversaries deliberately trigger thousands of benign alerts to poison sliding time windows and overwhelm LLM context windows.

### AegisHunt Defensive Mitigation:
**Behavioral MITRE ATT&CK Matrix Alignment:**  
Rather than relying on static token signatures or keyword matches, AegisHunt maps activity to foundational MITRE tactics (e.g. T1047 Remote WMI, T1558.003 Kerberoasting). Even if process names are obfuscated, parent-child socket relationships and service ticket requests trigger behavioral detection rules.

---

## 4. Context Window Truncation and APT Dwell Times

### The Vulnerability:
Modern APT campaigns frequently exhibit dwell times of 60 to 200 days, pausing for weeks between credential harvesting and exfiltration. Feeding months of raw SIEM logs (terabytes of text) into an LLM context window exceeds token budgets and introduces recency decay, causing the model to miss early reconnaissance phases.

### AegisHunt Defensive Mitigation:
**Hierarchical Graph Abstraction:**  
The platform reduces billions of telemetry tokens into compact directed graph topologies (Nodes = Entities, Edges = Causality links). The AI evaluates the distilled topological graph structure rather than uncompressed raw log lines.

---

## 5. Lack of Business Context & Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

### The Vulnerability:
An AI cannot discern internal business intent. For example:
- A penetration tester conducting an authorized red-team exercise looks identical to an active adversary.
- An IT backup script terminating services looks identical to ransomware defense evasion.
Autonomous remediation by an AI without human confirmation can knock out core production databases.

### AegisHunt Defensive Mitigation:
**Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Governance:**  
AI acts strictly as a Decision Support System (DSS). It proposes hypotheses, correlates chains, and suggests containment steps, but all destructive actions (host isolation, credential revocation, token cancellation) require explicit sign-off by a certified human analyst.
